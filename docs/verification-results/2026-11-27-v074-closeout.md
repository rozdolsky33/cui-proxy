## Capacity discovery and the passing tier

The [original workload contract](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-daily-use-workload-contract.md) makes eight/sixteen-client latency and throughput report-only capacity discovery; safety remains mandatory. The four-client normal tier is the release performance gate. Completing the exploratory attempts does not turn their strict benchmark failures into passes.

The unchanged image extractor admits two active operations and two queued operations. All exploratory proxy warmups stopped after uncached-image bursts produced HTTP 422 refusals. A representative eight-request native diagnostic identified recovery code `attachment_image_busy` in all four refusal responses; individual response bodies for the benchmark refusals were not retained. The source admission budget supports this attribution, but is not independent proof of every refusal code. The strict benchmark warmups stopped before matched proxy measurements could be collected. This establishes an uncached-image burst limitation, not a global four-client connection or text-throughput ceiling.

Keep client concurrency and attachment-heavy bursts within the measured supported envelope. Reduce simultaneous uncached image work and retry a refused operation after existing extraction finishes. Do not raise safety budgets to manufacture a passing result or infer successful higher-concurrency measurements from these refusals.

The initial roadmap's correctness and recovery promise is now supported by exact-candidate workflow and native recovery evidence.
